# Roadmap

Pending features, prioritised by effort vs impact. Priority tags: `P0`
(live/broken, fix now) → `P3` (low urgency, do when convenient); `Icebox`
items are deliberately unranked against P0-P3, not yet refined enough for
a priority call. Deferred items and Open decisions aren't tagged — they're
gated on something else (a scoping decision, a track resuming), not ready
to be worked regardless of priority.

Every non-`Deferred` item also carries two more tags, self-assessed by the
agent, not guarantees: `Autonomy` — how much of the task can be executed
without a mid-task decision from you, once you give a go-ahead. `Full`: no
decisions needed from you at all. `High`: nearly all of it is mine, but one
point needs your input (a judgment call, a content review, a choice between
close options). `Low`: needs substantial ongoing judgment or hands-on work
from you throughout — I can scaffold, instruct, or review, not drive it
alone. `None`: blocked on a decision only you can make; no execution path
exists yet. `Tokens` — a rough order-of-magnitude estimate of the total
conversation cost for an agent session to complete the task end-to-end
(tool output included) — a loose planning guide, not a quote; items still
blocked on a decision are marked `N/A` since nothing is scoped to estimate
yet. `Deferred` items aren't tagged — they aren't being worked at all right
now, so there's nothing to assess. A `[Shipped]` item (kept here briefly for
recent-history context, then removable) is also untagged for the same
reason — it's already done, not pending.

## Ideas / Icebox
Nascent product ideas — not yet refined into epics or stories, not prioritised against the P0-P3 work above (deliberately — they aren't ready for a priority call yet). Captured so they aren't lost.
- `[Icebox]` `[Autonomy: None]` `[Tokens: N/A, blocked]` **Partizip II for verbs** — teach past participle forms (e.g. `gemacht`, `gegangen`, separable-verb participles like `aufgestanden`); how to teach it is unexplored. Also surface which auxiliary (`haben`/`sein`) each verb takes for Perfekt — needed alongside Partizip II, not derivable from the participle alone. Build-order hint: Adjective degrees (below) may be a good first pass at the "teach a grammatical form + test it" pattern, since Partizip II likely needs the same content-module + exercise-type shape.
- `[Icebox]` `[Autonomy: Low]` `[Tokens: ~250k once scoped]` **Adjective degrees (Komparation)** — teach comparison forms (Positiv/Komparativ/Superlativ: `schön → schöner → am schönsten`, including irregulars like `gut → besser → am besten`); needs a content module plus a testable exercise type. Candidate first build for the content-module + exercise-type pattern Partizip II would also need.
- `[Icebox]` `[Autonomy: None]` `[Tokens: N/A, blocked]` **Verbs as a standalone theme** — tag/group verbs (all levels, starting at A1) as their own study theme, separate from the mixed CEFR-level content, so a user can drill verbs specifically. Possibly bundle Perfekt-form teaching directly into verb introduction rather than as a separate drill (overlaps with Partizip II, above). Open sub-idea: tiering verbs by difficulty within the theme — trennbar (separable) verbs are already effectively the hardest category; a regular/weak → strong/irregular → trennbar progression might be the natural tiering.
- `[Icebox]` `[Autonomy: High]` `[Tokens: ~200k]` **Show full verb conjugation (Konjugation)** — a reference table (all persons, at least present tense) rather than a graded exercise; link to it from verb word-detail pages.
- `[Icebox]` `[Autonomy: None]` `[Tokens: N/A, blocked]` **Custom Lists** — user-curated word lists outside the fixed CEFR-level content (e.g. a self-made "Bürgeramt visit" list), with their own study/session scoping.
- `[Icebox]` `[Autonomy: Low]` `[Tokens: ~150k once scoped]` **Related terms / compound words section** — a new content module for words related to the target word by compounding rather than synonymy (`Rente` → `Altersrente`, `Tisch` → `Esstisch`), i.e. the hyponym relationship the `similarWords` fix (see In-flight / Tech debt) deliberately excluded. Distinct module from `similarWords` since the relationship is "narrower/specific-instance," not "same concept" — needs its own heading, data shape, and design principle before use.
- `[Icebox]` `[Autonomy: None]` `[Tokens: N/A, blocked]` **Confusable-words drilling** — a module/exercise teaching DaF learners to tell apart words that look or sound alike but aren't interchangeable, where mixing them up is a known learner error: `zahlen`/`bezahlen`, `werden`/`würden` (and possibly `wurden`, if all three are commonly confused as legal-looking infinitive/conjugated forms — verify), `wenn`/`wann`. Different problem from both `similarWords` (same concept) and the compound-words idea above (narrower concept): here the words are often unrelated in meaning and the teaching goal is contrast, not similarity. Needs its own content shape and exercise type — unexplored.
- `[Icebox]` `[Autonomy: High]` `[Tokens: ~80k]` **Auto-advance "Next" button on exercises, with a configurable countdown** — after any exercise is answered (correct or incorrect), show a "Next" button below it that auto-advances after 3-5 seconds if the user doesn't click it first; the remaining time renders as a clockwise-sweeping ring/clock animation around the button's border rather than a numeric countdown. The countdown duration should be user-configurable in Settings, following the existing `localStorage`-backed settings pattern (`flip_animation`/`mobile_full_view`/`hardcore_mode`/`session_timeout`). Unexplored: interaction with `hardcoreMode`, whether wrong answers should get a longer window than correct ones (more time to read the correction), and whether auto-advance should be disable-able entirely (0 = off) rather than just tuned within a range.
- `[Icebox]` `[Autonomy: Low]` `[Tokens: ~40k once scoped]` **Configurable default session length** — a Settings control for how long/large a session is by default, so the user doesn't have to re-pick it every time. Distinct from the existing `SessionTimeoutSection`/`readSessionTimeout`/`writeSessionTimeout` (that's an *inactivity* timeout that ends a stalled session, not a target session length/word-count) — needs its own setting, and needs "session length" to first be defined precisely (a word/exercise count? a duration? see the session-length control already surfaced at session start, if any) before this is buildable.
- `[Icebox]` `[Autonomy: None]` `[Tokens: N/A, blocked]` **Theme/category completion badges** — badges/achievements for completing a whole grammatical or thematic category (e.g. all `reflexiv` verbs, all `trennbar` verbs) or hitting a word-count milestone within a theme, surfaced alongside the existing streak mechanic. Overlaps with the "A2 verbs as a standalone theme" idea above (both need per-theme progress tracking) — needs a decision on what counts as "complete" and how progress is computed before this is buildable.
- `[Shipped]` **Advanced difficulty tier for `ContextBlankQuestion` on reflexive verbs, unlocked after mastery** — PR #107 (2026-09-30), on top of the earlier baseline fix (force all four options reflexive for a reflexive target — PR #99). Underlying leak: a reflexive verb's `paragraphWithUsage` only blanked the verb, leaving the pronoun (`uns`/`mich`/`dich`/`sich`/`euch`) visible next to the blank, and options showed the dictionary form including "sich" — together letting a learner spot the correct option by pattern-matching "has sich," with zero vocabulary knowledge. Shipped: once a reflexive word is mastered (same gating threshold `TypeTheWordQuestion` uses, `Math.round(stats.count * stats.accuracy) >= 3`, in `sessionAlgorithm.ts`'s `generateExerciseData`), the pronoun is now also blanked — as its own separate `{{...}}` span, placed at its real position in each sentence (never assumed adjacent; German word order, e.g. Perfekt, often separates pronoun and verb) — and "sich" is stripped from every option label. Below mastery, behavior is unchanged from the baseline. Found and fixed along the way: `ContextBlankQuestion.tsx`'s live preview echoed the same full selected-option text in every active blank, which broke once a sentence could have two live blanks at once (pronoun + verb) — fixed by mapping each blank to its own word by position within the sentence.
- `[Icebox]` `[Autonomy: Low]` `[Tokens: ~600k+ once scoped]` **Toggleable English translation of context sentences** — surfaced while auditing verb coverage against a standard A1–B1 verb list (before the app had an A1 tier — the app now has A1 as its floor, see "Word content scale" above): at the lowest active level, reading `wordContext.paragraphWithUsage` sentences with zero translation aid is genuinely hard for some learners — the gap gets worse the closer to the floor a learner actually is. Idea: an optional English translation shown alongside (or revealed on demand for) each context sentence, toggleable both from a global Settings switch and inline from the exercise/card itself, default state persisted to `localStorage` the same way `flip_animation`/`mobile_full_view`/`hardcore_mode` already are. Open questions before this is buildable:
  - **Data**: `paragraphWithUsage` has no translation field today — needs either a parallel `paragraphTranslations: string[]` array (one entry per sentence, kept in sync by hand) or some other shape, plus writing an actual translation for every sentence across every word file (mock/data content work, not just a schema change).
  - **Scope**: the passive `WordContext` card (just reading, translation aid is unambiguously helpful) is different from `ContextBlankQuestion` (an active recall/guessing exercise) — showing the translation *before* answering there could hand the learner the blanked word's meaning and make the exercise trivial. Likely needs to be WordContext-only, or gated to reveal only after answering in ContextBlankQuestion, not a single on/off switch covering both uniformly.
- `[Icebox]` `[Autonomy: Low]` `[Tokens: ~300k+ once scoped]` **ElevenLabs pronunciation audio in the main app** — `sites/pronunciation-poc` (PR #102) already proved out the ElevenLabs text-to-speech API end-to-end: server-side API key handling (never exposed client-side), a voice picker, and a playback-speed control (`SteppedSlider`, now a shared component). It's currently a standalone demo site, not wired into Crosslex itself. Idea: let learners hear correct pronunciation of vocabulary words (and/or context sentences) directly from word cards/exercises. Needs real scoping before buildable: **cost** (ElevenLabs calls aren't free at the word-content scale this app already has — live TTS per play vs. pre-generating and caching audio per word is a real cost/latency tradeoff); **where audio lives** (generated once and stored as static assets alongside `mock/data/`, vs. generated on demand); **UX placement** (just `WordIntro`? every `wordContext` example sentence too?); and whether this is worth the added infra for an alpha-stage product versus a nice-to-have polish item.
